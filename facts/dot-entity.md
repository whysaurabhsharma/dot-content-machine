# Dot, in Dot's own words

Hard rule 23: a piece, a post, a bio or a profile describes Dot only in the words below, never paraphrased. They are quoted from Dot's own home page; each line gives where it sits.

## The entity paragraph

Use it whole, or use any one sentence of it whole:

> Dot, your AI Data Analyst. Dot answers data questions for your team. Get insights where you work: Slack, Microsoft Teams, and email.

| "Get insights where you work: Slack, Microsoft Teams, and email." | https://www.getdot.ai/, under the "Trusted by 100+ top teams" logos |

Read 31 October 2026 with WebFetch, which returns a model's reading of the page, not its raw text: a person should check the three sentences against the live page before this file is relied on.

## How it sits with hard rule 5

These sentences are Dot's description of itself, quoted from its own site. A piece may use them as how Dot describes Dot, with no further evidence. They are not a test result: a piece that relies on one as a tested fact (for example that Dot works in Microsoft Teams) still needs evidence, a yes from Dot, or an "Assumption" label (rule 5).

## Not used

- The page title, "Dot · Your Data Team, Scaled by AI": a tagline, not a definition.
- "Empower everyone to get instant, actionable insights.": a promise, not a definition.
- The About page: it states Dot's purpose but never defines Dot in a sentence.
- "Snowboard Software GmbH d/b/a GetDot" (the legal entity, in the footer): use only where the legal name is needed.
